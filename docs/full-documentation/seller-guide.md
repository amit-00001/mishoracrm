# SaaS CRM — Sales & Reseller Guide

**Audience:** Anyone selling this platform to a prospective business — resellers, sales staff, partners.

Use this to understand what you're selling, who to pitch it to, and how to answer the questions prospects actually ask.

---

## 1. The 30-Second Pitch

> "One CRM that adapts to your business — whether you sell products, manufacture goods, or run a service business, it's the same platform, and you only pay for the pieces you actually use."

Most CRMs are built for one kind of business and force everyone else to make do. This platform ships **three business models in one codebase** — a general sales CRM core, a full Manufacturing/Purchasing suite, and a complete Service-Business suite (subscriptions, bookings, time billing, helpdesk) — switched on or off per customer through their plan. A gym, an IT AMC company, a coaching institute, and a small manufacturer can all run on the same platform without any of them seeing features irrelevant to them.

## 3. Who To Target First — Ranked

The strongest reason a small business buys this platform is **WhatsApp messaging that does itself** — bulk offers, plus automatic reminders for renewals, appointments and fees. So the ranking below is by how well a prospect fits that, using four tests:

1. They already hold a customer or enquiry list with phone numbers.
2. They have a *recurring* reason to message that list (renewals, appointments, fees, offers).
3. The owner decides alone, so the sale is quick.
4. The plan price is easy to justify against what they lose today.

This ranking is a judgement based on product fit, not measured conversion data. Adjust it as real results come in.

### Don't chase these for now

- **Cold-list blasters** — anyone who wants to message numbers that never opted in. Messages go through the official WhatsApp Business API, which needs opt-in; their number can get blocked.
- **Categories WhatsApp prohibits** — gambling, crypto, MLM, adult.
- **Banks, NBFCs, hospital chains and other large enterprises** — long procurement and compliance. Take them only if they come to us, through *Talk to sales*.
- **Very small single-owner shops** (a lone kirana, for example) — the budget isn't there.
- **Anyone who needs one-click blasts to tens of thousands of contacts** — see §9.

### How to open the conversation

Ask three questions, and let the answers pick the pitch:

1. "How do you remind customers about renewals, appointments or fees today?"
2. "How many customers or enquiries have you got phone numbers for?"
3. "Who sends those messages — you, from your own phone?"

A prospect who answers "me, from my phone, one by one" is a ready buyer.

### 1. Gyms, yoga, dance and sports academies

**Their problem today:** Renewals live in a register or Excel, so members lapse unnoticed. Enquiries from Instagram and Meta ads sit unanswered for hours.

**Opening line:** "How do you know a membership is about to expire — and who tells the member?"

**Sell this:**
- **Service Subscriptions** (Pro) — one record per membership with start and expiry, automatic expiry reminders by email and WhatsApp in the owner's own wording, one-click or bulk renewal, and optional auto-renew that raises a draft invoice.
- **Bulk WhatsApp** — festival offers, new-batch announcements and "bring a friend" campaigns to the whole member list.
- **Lead capture** — Meta and Instagram Lead Ads enquiries arrive in the CRM, are auto-assigned to the least-busy staff member and set follow-up reminders.
- **Appointments** — trial-session and class booking by link.
- **Invoices** — GST invoices with part-payment tracking for fee instalments.

**Demo in 10 minutes:** import the member list from Excel → add a membership plan → show the expiry reminder → send one bulk offer to ten members.

**Don't promise:** automatic card or UPI charging of members, entry-gate or biometric check-in, trainer-wise scheduling.

### 2. Coaching, tuition, skill and computer institutes, playschools

**Their problem today:** Ad and JustDial enquiries get one or two calls and go cold. Fee follow-up is by phone and parents are messaged one by one.

**Opening line:** "Of the people who enquired last admission season, how many did you follow up after the first week?"

**Sell this:**
- **Lead capture** — Meta Ads, JustDial and Sulekha enquiries land in one list, are auto-assigned, and raise an alert if nobody contacts them in time.
- **Appointments** — demo-class or counselling booking by link, with reminders and no-show tracking.
- **Bulk WhatsApp** — batch-timing and holiday notices to parents, fee-due messages, results, and an admission offer to old enquiries with the brochure or fee structure attached (PDF or image, up to 16 MB).
- **Invoices** — fee instalments as part-payments; overdue alerts go to the staff member who raised the invoice.
- **Service Subscriptions** (Pro) — track each course term with expiry reminders.

**Demo in 10 minutes:** import the enquiry list → send an admission message with the brochure → book a demo class through the link on your own phone.

**Don't promise:** a school ERP — there is no timetable, student attendance, exam or report-card module. Invoice overdue alerts go to staff, not to parents, so fee reminders to parents go out as bulk WhatsApp messages.

### 3. Salons, spas, dental, skin and physio clinics

**Their problem today:** No-shows leave empty slots, customers come once and never return, and booking is a WhatsApp back-and-forth.

**Opening line:** "How many bookings last month didn't turn up, and how many customers haven't been back in 60 days?"

- **Customer Loyalty** (Business) — points or stamps, tiers, birthday and anniversary rewards, and a win-back campaign aimed at customers who haven't visited in N days.

- **Invoices** — GST invoices.

**Demo in 10 minutes:** set up the booking link → book a test slot from your phone → show the reminder → create a "not visited in 60 days" win-back campaign (Business plan).

**Don't promise:** a calendar per stylist or doctor (slots are capacity-based), or clinic medical records, prescriptions and lab reports.

### 4. AC, RO, solar, CCTV and IT service / AMC companies

## 6. How Plans & Pricing Work

Every customer subscribes to a **Plan**. Each plan defines:
- The number of team members (a fixed seat count). Leads and contacts are **always unlimited** — there is no lead cap on any plan.
- Which optional modules are switched on: WhatsApp, Advanced Reports, Social Media Leads, Third-party Lead Sources, Manufacturing, Service Catalog, Subscriptions, Appointments/Booking, Time Tracking, Tickets/Helpdesk, Customer Loyalty, Customer Portal.
- Monthly or yearly pricing (with an automatic discount for annual commitment).

The five standard plans are **Free**, **Starter** (₹15,000/yr), **Pro** (₹23,999/yr), **Business** (₹49,999/yr) and **Enterprise**. The paid plans start with a 14-day free trial. These are the standard list prices — check the live Pricing page before quoting, because the platform team can change them.

**WhatsApp campaigns are not on the Free plan.** They start at Starter, so a prospect who wants bulk messaging cannot stay on Free.

Enterprise is a *custom* plan: it carries no fixed price and no self-serve checkout — the pricing page shows "Custom" and a **Talk to sales** button that opens the public `/contact-sales` form. Those enquiries land in **Super Admin → Sales Enquiries** (and are emailed to the address set there).

**This means you can position plans by vertical, not just by size:**
- A "Starter" plan with just the CRM core suits anyone getting started.
- A "Service Pro" plan adds Subscriptions + Appointments + Time Tracking + Tickets for service businesses.
- A "Manufacturing" plan adds the Purchasing/Work Order suite.
- Individual customers can also get one-off module overrides from the platform side if they need something outside their plan temporarily (e.g. a trial of Tickets before upgrading) — a flexible upsell lever.

Coupons/discount codes are also supported for promotions.

## 8. Objection Handling

- **"We already use WhatsApp/Excel for this."** — That's exactly the gap: nothing reminds itself, nothing tracks itself, and nothing survives someone leaving the team. This platform's whole value is doing the remembering and reminding automatically.
- **"Is it only for [gym / manufacturing / IT]?"** — No — it's one platform, and you only turn on (and pay for) the modules relevant to your business. A trading company, a service business, and a mixed business (sells products *and* offers services) can all run on it.
- **"Can our customers use it without installing anything?"** — Yes. Quotation approval, appointment booking, and support ticket submission are all link-based, no login, no app — this is a deliberate design choice, not a limitation.
- **"What if we need something custom?"** — Two paths: (1) custom fields and custom roles are self-service, no developer needed; (2) for a bespoke automated workflow, there's a request-based automation service where the platform team builds it for you.
- **"Can we control who on our team sees what?"** — Yes, granular role-based permissions, fully customizable per tenant.

## 9. What NOT To Oversell

Be accurate — a prospect who's disappointed after signing is worse than a slower close:

- "AI Automation" in the product is a **request form for a custom build**, not a drag-and-drop automation builder they can use themselves the moment they sign up.
- A customer login exists only as the **Customer Portal / Wallet** (loyalty points and stamps across shops, Business plan). Everything else customer-facing — a quotation, an appointment, a ticket — is a link per record, not a dashboard where the customer sees all of their history.
- Subscription renewal needs a staff member to click "Renew" after the reminder goes out, unless that subscription is set to auto-renew. Auto-renew extends the term and creates a draft invoice; it does not collect the payment, so it is not a recurring billing engine for the tenant's *own* customers.
- Appointments are capacity-based (X people per slot), not "book a specific staff member" — if a prospect specifically needs per-technician scheduling, flag it rather than promise it.

**Bulk WhatsApp — say these out loud before the prospect assumes otherwise:**

- **It is not a cold-list blaster.** Messages go through the official WhatsApp Business API, to people who have opted in — existing customers and people who enquired. Sending to bought or scraped lists risks the prospect's number being blocked.
- **Approved templates.** A message to someone who hasn't messaged the business in the last 24 hours generally has to be a WhatsApp-approved template. Before you promise a campaign, confirm with the platform team which WhatsApp connection the prospect will be on and whether approved templates are available on it.
- **Automatic WhatsApp reminders.** Subscription-expiry and appointment reminders currently go out as plain WhatsApp text, not as approved templates, so WhatsApp may not deliver them to a customer who hasn't messaged the business recently. Email reminders are not affected. Test on a real number before you promise WhatsApp reminders in a demo or a quote.
- **Size.** Bulk send is built for lists of hundreds, not tens of thousands. For more than about a thousand recipients in one send, check with the platform team first.
- **Choosing recipients.** The bulk screen lists leads or contacts to tick, with a search box. Targeting by behaviour — spend, visits, inactive for N days, tier — belongs to Loyalty campaigns, which are on the Business plan.
- **Message charges.** Confirm how WhatsApp message charges are billed before quoting, and never describe messaging as "unlimited and free".

## 10. Closing Checklist

1. Identify the prospect's business type → find it in the ranking in §3 and map to the relevant feature set.
2. Identify their current pain (spreadsheets, missed renewals, lost complaints, no visibility into stock) → tie it directly to the automation that solves it.
3. Recommend a plan tier that matches which modules they actually need — don't oversell modules they won't use.
4. Offer a walkthrough of the one or two features that matter most to them, not a tour of everything.
5. Set expectations correctly using §9 above.
